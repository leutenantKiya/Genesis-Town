### The kit (`client/js/three/`, global `window.GT3`)

Units: 1 unit = 1 tile, 1 art pixel = `GT3.PX` (1/12). Tile (x, y) covers x..x+1 on X and y..y+1 on Z. Y points up.
The camera sits in the south, so fronts face +Z.

| File | What it gives |
| --- | --- |
| `kit.js` | `GT3.tex` (pixel texture), `GT3.mat` (banded toon material: every lit thing uses it), `GT3.merge` (flat-shaded vertex-coloured merge), `quad`, `xform`, `tileCanvas(type)` (a 2D tile sprite without its grass), `crop`, `dispose`, `pal()` / `season()` / `snowy()` (the live ramps in `art.js` `S.PAL`) |
| `ground.js` | `GT3.Ground(L)`: the 2D ground art on one plane, water sunk 3 px with bank walls, the cut soil edge, mist over locked chunks. Has `redraw()` and `locked(x, y)` |
| `building.js` | `GT3.Building(b)`: walls with the 2D facade (cropped below its painted roof), a gabled roof, plus per-kind extras (tower, flag, awning, chimney). `userData`: `{ building, mats, box, door, height, glow(k) }` |
| `nature.js` | `GT3.Nature(L)`: instanced trees (oak, fruit, pine, bare in winter), rocks and bushes, and the tall grass and flowers as crossed sprite cards. Has `recolor()` and `fade(tree, a)` (a ghost copy is drawn while faded) |
| `props.js` | `GT3.Props(L)`: fences, signs, mailboxes, lamps, barrels, crates, stalls with awnings, the portal. Has `glow(k)` |
| `actor.js` | `GT3.Actor(palette)`: the 2D sprite on a card that faces the camera and leans back by its pitch (upright cards looked like squashed heads), plus a ground shadow. Has `set(x, z, dir, step)` and `face(camera)` |
| `sky.js` | `GT3.Sky(scene)`: sun (casts shadows, east to west), hemisphere fill and fog, set by hour and season; `night` 0..1 |
| `rig.js` | `GT3.Rig(camera)`: follow camera, fov 28, pitch 52 degrees, eased. Has `zoom`, `turn` and `snap` |
| `seethrough.js` | `GT3.SeeThrough(world)`: a building fades to 40% when you are behind it, a tree crown to 45%; times follow DESIGN.md |
| `world.js` | `GT3.World(L)`: all of the above for one layout. Has `walkable(x, y)`, `update(t, hour, night)` and `setSeason()` |

## Task 1: walk into a building with no cut (owner chose this, 2026-11-10)

The owner wants the player to walk in and see the room without a scene cut. The problem is that **rooms are bigger
than buildings**, so simply fading the roof shows a room that does not fit:

| | Outside footprint | Inside room (engine.js) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House | 3 x 3 | 10 x 8 (`genHouseInterior`) |
| Public building | 4-5 x 3-5 | 12 x 9 (`genPublicInterior`) |
| Mart, casino | | 11 x 9 (`genMartInterior`, `genCasinoInterior`) |

**Decision: "the room unfolds".** Making buildings as big as their rooms would change `village.js` and `homeland.js`,
which the server uses to check positions; there is not enough time before the deadline. So the room stays its own
scene, as the server and the 2D game know it, and only the 2.5D view animates the change, in about 0.7 s with no
black frame:

1. **Trigger:** the player stands on the tile below a door (`b.dx, b.dy + 1`) and moves up. In the game, use the
   engine's own door enter event instead.
2. **Fade the building:** roof and walls go from 1 to 0 over the first 45% of the move. Hide it (`visible = false`)
   while the player is inside, and turn the see-through pass off.
3. **Unfold the room:** the room group is anchored at the door.
   - The doormat centre `(mat.x + 0.5, m.h - 0.5)` in room space sits on `(b.dx + 0.5, b.dy + 0.5)` in world space.
   - Scale goes from `min(b.w / m.w, b.h / m.h)` to 1 between 15% and 100% of the move.
   - Room alpha goes from 0 to 1 between 5% and 40%.
   - At scale 1, world = room + `(b.dx - mat.x, b.dy - (m.h - 1))`.
4. **Mist the village:** draw four large flat planes around a rectangular hole, at y = 1.75 (just above the room's
   walls).
   - Material: `MeshBasicMaterial`, transparent, `depthWrite: false`, `fog: false`, opacity 0 to 1.
   - Colour: a mix of `--hud` and the sky.
   - The hole follows the scaled room, with about 0.6 tiles of margin.
   - Move the hole toward the camera by `1.75 / tan(52 degrees)`, about 1.37 tiles in +Z, because the camera looks
     down at an angle.
   - Hide the world group once the mist is fully opaque.
5. **Player and camera:** move the player from outside the door to `m.entry` in world space over the same time. Zoom
   the rig to about 1.35. Inside, walk on the room's own tiles (wall ring, furniture is solid).
6. **Leave:** stepping onto the doormat while moving down plays it all in reverse. The player ends at
   `(b.dx + 0.5, b.dy + 1.5)`. Give the building back to the see-through pass at alpha 1.
7. **Reduced motion** (`html.gt-reduced-motion` or `prefers-reduced-motion`): jump to the end state. The room still
   shows, because the change itself is functional.
8. **Light:** a warm `PointLight` at the room centre, height about 1.4, stronger at night (`sky.night`). Tune it from a
   screenshot.

### New file `client/js/three/room.js`: `GT3.Room(m, style)`

- Input: an interior map shaped like the engine's: `{ w, h, tiles[], mat: {x, y}, entry: {x, y} }`.
- `style`: wall colours from `PUBLIC_STYLE[kind]` or `HOUSE_STYLES[style]` (`wall`, `wallD`).
- Returns `{ group, walkable(x, y), mats (for alpha), entry, mat }`.
- **Floor:** one canvas of `w*12 x h*12`.
  - `floor_wood` is drawn as planks, `floor_tile` as a checker with grout, `floor_dark` from `FB_COL` in `engine.js`
    (around line 108).
  - Flat tiles are painted onto the floor canvas with `SPRITES.drawOverlay`: `rug`, `doormat`, and `carpet_red` (from
    `FB_COL`).
- **Walls:**
  - The wall ring tiles become 1-tile boxes, 1.7 high.
  - Sides use a wallpaper texture: the wall colour, a `wallD` wainscot on the lower 40% and a trim line.
  - The top is the ink cap `#2a2430`, which reads as a cut-away dollhouse section.
  - The front row (`y = h - 1`) is only 0.3 high, with a gap at the doormat, so the camera sees in.
- **Furniture:** one box per tile, extruding the 2D sprite (`GT3.tileCanvas(type)`).
  - Tall pieces carry the sprite on the front (+Z) face: `fridge` 1.3, `bookshelf` 1.3, `shelf` 1.1, `tv` 0.8,
    `slot` 1.2.
  - Low pieces carry it on the top face: `bed` 0.45, `table` 0.55, `counter` 0.75, `chest` 0.45, `chair` 0.35,
    `dice_table` / `card_table` 0.6 (colours from `FB_COL`).
  - Side faces take the sprite's average colour times 0.75.
  - `plant` is two crossed cards, 0.9 high. Anything unknown is a 0.5 box in grey.
- **Where the rooms come from:**
  - The engine's generators are private (`houseScene(b)` caches them in the `interiors` map). Add a small
    `GT.interiorFor(b)` to `engine.js` so the 3D view gets the same map the 2D game uses.
  - For the lab, copy the house and public layouts into a `GT3.demoRoom(b)`, marked as lab-only.
- The 2D sprites that exist: `wall, rug, doormat, bed, table, tv, plant, shelf, counter, fridge, bookshelf, chair, chest,
  slot, lamp`. The floors, `carpet_red`, `dice_table` and `card_table` have no sprite; the engine draws them from
  `FB_COL`.

### New file `client/js/three/walkin.js`: `GT3.WalkIn(world, scene, rig, sky)`

## Task 2 (after Task 1): the 2.5D view in the game

- A Pengaturan setting "Tampilan: 2D / 2.5D". The 2D view stays the default and the fallback when WebGL is missing.
- Load `vendor/three.js` and `js/three/*` lazily, only for 2.5D, the way `r3d/boot.js` on the other branch does.
- The engine keeps all game state and server calls. The 3D view only reads them:
  - player position and direction, other players and NPCs (`players.js` presence);
  - `GT.world.layout`, `worldId` (`public` or `HL-<n>`) and the clock and season;
  - lots and Homeland buildings as they change.
- DOM overlays (HUD, windows, hotbar, chat) stay as they are. Canvas overlays (name tags, bubbles, floating numbers,
  fishing, fights) need a 3D-to-screen projection, or a 2D canvas laid over the WebGL canvas.
